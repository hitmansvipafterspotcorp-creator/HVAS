First hour, in order

Do this
  1. 01Set up Cash App and Venmo if you don't have them. Cash App money is spendable the second it lands. Do not use Stripe or Square this week — a new account holds your first payout for a week or more, which is exactly the week you don't have.
  2. 02Build the example page for a business you can see from where you're standing. Takes six minutes. Now you have a demo.
  3. 03Save this page to your home screen so it opens like an app and works with no signal.
  4. 04Walk into ten places today. Ten, not three. Two out of ten is a normal day and two is $118.
  5. 05Build the page while they watch. That's the whole trick — nobody says no to something already finished with their name on it.

What you say

30 seconds

"Hey — quick question, are you the owner? I make one-page websites for local businesses, and I do it right here on my phone in about ten minutes. Can I show you yours? No charge to look."

Then stop talking and start typing. Ask them the questions off Sheet 1 out loud — name, what you do, your number, your prices. Get their hours. Ask for the nicest thing a customer ever said. Then turn the phone around.

"That's live right now. It'll open on any phone, it's got your Call button, your prices, your hours. It's $59 and I'll text it to you before I walk out."

Then be quiet. Whoever talks first, loses. If they hesitate: "I'll do it for $40 if you can pay right now — Cash App, Venmo, or cash."

Where to walk

  • Food trucksNear FAMU on South Adams and Wahnish Way, the lots off Tennessee St, and any brewery lot. Catch them at setup, ~30 minutes before they open, not during a rush.
  • Mobile detailers & car washesTennessee St and Capital Circle. High ticket, no website, ready to book by text.
  • Lawn & landscape crewsThe Home Depot and Lowe's lots between 6:30 and 8am. Cash business, cash buyers.
  • Nail techs, lash techs, hair stylistsSouthside and the strip centers on Apalachee Pkwy. A prices-and-booking page is exactly what they're missing.
  • Flea market and weekend vendorsWhole rows of them in one place. Best conversations-per-hour in town.
  • Railroad Square, First FridayArtists and makers, all in a walkable block, all in a buying mood.
  • Movers, haulers, handymen, cleanersAnybody advertising on a Facebook group with no link. Message them, then meet in person.

Go where they're already standing still. A vendor at a booth cannot walk away from you, and has nothing to do while waiting for customers. That's the best sales environment you will find for free.

How this scales

After the 9th
  1. 01Week 2 — raise the price. Once five people have paid $59, charge $99. Show the five pages you already built as proof.
  2. 02Week 3 — sell the monthly. Go back to everyone you built for: "$19 a month and I keep it current." Ten of those is $190 arriving every month whether you work or not.
  3. 03Week 4 — pick one trade. Do nothing but barbershops, or nothing but food trucks. Your pitch gets sharper and referrals start moving inside that trade on their own.
  4. 04Month 2 — stop knocking. Every page you built carries a small "Built by [your name] — 850-xxx-xxxx" line at the bottom. That line sells pages while you sleep. Your name and number are already on it.
  5. 05Month 3 — hand off the walking. Someone else knocks and builds, you keep $20 of every $59. That's when it stops being a job.
